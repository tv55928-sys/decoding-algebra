For teachers

Teach students to read algebra before they manipulate it.

Many errors in algebra are reading errors: a missed bracket, a sign left behind, an exponent read as a coefficient. Decoding is the habit of reading an expression's symbols, structure and meaning before doing anything to it. This app shows you how to teach that habit explicitly.

    Teach the routine

    Introduce one step per lesson in a 10–15 minute mini-lesson: model it, practise it together, then assign a Practice activity. After six lessons, run the whole routine as a warm-up on the expression of the day.

    Model reading like a mathematician

    Say these habits aloud as you model. They come from how mathematicians described their own reading in .

    Reread. “I'll read it twice: once to find the parts, again to see how they fit.”
    Every symbol counts. “One bracket changes the meaning: 3(x + 2) and 3x + 2 are different.”
    Know your letters. “Before I start: x stands for a number.”
    Read to check. “Let me test this step by putting in a number.”

      Research behind these choices

      • Start concrete. Use physical algebra tiles before the virtual ones, then drawings, then symbols ().
      • Use precise language and revoice informal talk. Say “add to zero”, not “cancel”. Ask the reflective questions aloud as you think ().
      • Welcome everyday and home languages. Let students explain in any language, with gestures, drawings or tiles, then work toward the math register together ().
      • Teach brackets and ambiguity directly. Explicit lessons on grouping, brackets and ambiguity raised one school's success from 31% to 81% ().
      • Use incorrect examples. Have students find and explain errors, then compare with correct work ().
